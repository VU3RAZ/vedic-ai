Brihat Jataka of Varahamihira — Classical Principles of Natal Astrology
(Based on B. Suryanarain Rao translation and classical commentary tradition)

PART ONE: PLANETARY NATURE AND SIGNIFICATIONS (GRAHA SVABHAVA)

CHAPTER 1 — THE SUN (SURYA)

Nature: The Sun is Sattvic in quality — pure, self-luminous, and authoritative. Among the three gunas, it represents the sattvic impulse toward clarity, truth, and dharmic action.

Primary Significations: The Sun signifies the soul (atma), self (ahankara), vitality (prana), father (pita), kings and rulers, authority figures, government, power, prestige, fame, honor, status, and all matters related to the head of state or the head of any institution. The Sun is the significator (karaka) of the first house, the self, and the father.

Body Parts Ruled: The Sun rules the heart, the right eye (in males), the left eye (in females), the bones (especially the spine), and the general vitality or pranic force of the body. Diseases associated with the Sun include heart disorders, eye diseases (particularly right-eye ailments), fevers, inflammatory conditions, bile disorders (pitta imbalance), baldness, and bone-related ailments.

Professions: Governmental service, administration, military leadership, politics, positions of authority and power, medicine (as a governing force), gold trade, wool, and all fire-related occupations.

Relatives: The Sun signifies the father specifically, and by extension, grandfather and all father figures.

Substances and Materials: Gold, copper, wheat, saffron, and all things of reddish-orange color. The Sun rules fire, and all fiery substances fall under its domain.

Color: Reddish-orange, copper, or golden. The Sun's color is described in classical texts as the color of dawn — neither fully red nor fully gold but the blend of both.

Directional Strength: The Sun gains Dig Bala (directional strength) in the 10th house — the midheaven — which corresponds to the direction of South. The Sun is strongest when it culminates at noon.

Natural Friends: The Moon, Mars, and Jupiter are natural friends of the Sun. The Sun is enmical to Venus and Saturn, and neutral toward Mercury.

Own Signs: The Sun rules only one sign: Leo (Simha). It is extremely comfortable in this placement and produces its finest results here.

Exaltation: Aries (highest at 10 degrees). The Sun's exaltation in the first sign of the zodiac, the sign of initiative and fire, reflects the idea of solar energy at its most pure and unbounded.

Debilitation: Libra (deepest at 10 degrees). The Sun is uncomfortable in Venus's sign of partnership and balance, where individual ego-assertion is subordinated to diplomatic accommodation.

Moolatrikona: Leo (0-20 degrees). Beyond the first 20 degrees of Leo, the Sun is in its own sign but not in moolatrikona.

Periods: The Sun rules the Vimsottari dasa of 6 years. Solar hours and Sundays (Ravivar) belong to the Sun. The Sun's Kala (period within a day) occurs at sunrise.

CHAPTER 2 — THE MOON (CHANDRA)

Nature: The Moon is Sattvic but with a strong Rajasic quality — it is the mind (manas), ever-changing, receptive, and emotionally responsive. The Moon receives and reflects the light of the Sun, just as the mind receives and reflects consciousness.

Primary Significations: The Moon signifies the mind (manas), emotions, mother (mata), the public, masses, women in general, water, liquids, milk, white objects, silver, pearls, flowers, and all things that grow and change in a cyclical manner. The Moon is the karaka of the 4th house and the mother.

Body Parts Ruled: The Moon rules the left eye (in males), the right eye (in females), the breasts, the stomach and digestive system, the blood, all bodily fluids, the uterus, and the lymphatic system. Diseases include mental imbalance, emotional disorders, colds and congestion, stomach ailments, blood disorders, edema, and gynecological conditions.

Professions: Nursing, childcare, hospitality, public catering, navy, fishing, water-related trades, dairy, medicine (as a healing, nurturing force), silver trade, and all occupations involving the public.

Relatives: The Moon signifies the mother primarily, and by extension, maternal relatives, women in general, and children (especially daughters).

Substances and Materials: Water, milk, silver, pearls, lotus flowers, white cloth, rice, and all white or silvery substances.

Color: White, cream, or silver. The Moon's color is the color of moonlight — pure, cool, and luminous.

Directional Strength: The Moon gains Dig Bala in the 4th house — the nadir or IC — which corresponds to the direction of North. The Moon is strongest at the lowest culmination, symbolizing its connection to the deep waters of the unconscious.

Natural Friends: The Sun and Mercury are natural friends of the Moon. The Moon has no natural enemies (this is a unique classical position — the Moon is universally friendly to all planets). It is neutral toward Mars, Jupiter, Venus, and Saturn.

Own Sign: Cancer (Karka). The Moon's deepest expression is in its own watery, emotional, nurturing sign.

Exaltation: Taurus (Vrishabha), highest at 3 degrees. The Moon is exalted in the fixed earth sign, where its otherwise changeable nature finds stability, beauty, and grounded nurturing.

Debilitation: Scorpio (Vrischika), lowest at 3 degrees. The Moon is deeply uncomfortable in the intense, secretive, transformative energy of Scorpio, where emotional safety is replaced by depth and upheaval.

Moolatrikona: Taurus (4-30 degrees). The Moon's moolatrikona overlaps with its exaltation sign, acknowledging that the Moon is deeply at home in Taurus.

Periods: The Moon rules the Vimsottari dasa of 10 years. Mondays (Somavar) and the night hours belong to the Moon.

CHAPTER 3 — MARS (MANGALA / KUJA)

Nature: Mars is Tamasic in quality — aggressive, violent, intense, and related to the primal instinct for survival and territorial assertion. Mars is the planet of war, fire, energy, and blood.

Primary Significations: Mars signifies courage (vikrama), younger siblings (especially brothers — anuja), physical energy, blood, fire, weapons, the military, police, surgery, engineering, agriculture (as it rules the use of sharp implements and the land), accidents, violence, desire (kama), and all competitive endeavors. Mars is the karaka of the 3rd house (younger siblings, courage) and the 6th house (conflicts, enemies).

Body Parts Ruled: Mars rules the blood, bone marrow, muscles, right nostril (in males), the reproductive organs (in tandem with Venus), the bile (pitta), the forehead, and cuts and wounds. Diseases associated with Mars include fevers, inflammatory conditions, accidents, injuries from sharp objects, blood disorders, piles, ulcers, bile disorders, and injuries to the head, face, and reproductive system.

Professions: Military, police, surgery, dentistry, butchery, metallurgy (especially ironwork), engineering (particularly civil and military), athletics, and all fire-related and violence-adjacent professions.

Relatives: Mars signifies younger brothers (and by extension, younger siblings), and also paternal uncles in some classical assignments.

Substances and Materials: Iron, steel, coral, red sandalwood, red cloth, copper (shared with the Sun), fire, and all sharp implements.

Color: Red (bright, blood-red). Mars's color is the intense red of blood and fire.

Directional Strength: Mars gains Dig Bala in the 10th house — the same as the Sun — representing the assertion of active, directed, outward-moving energy at its peak.

Natural Friends: The Sun, Moon, and Jupiter are natural friends of Mars. Mars is enmical to Mercury. It is neutral toward Venus and Saturn.

Own Signs: Mars rules Aries (Mesha) and Scorpio (Vrischika). In Aries, Mars expresses its extroverted, pioneering, aggressive qualities. In Scorpio, Mars expresses its internalized, intense, investigative qualities.

Exaltation: Capricorn (Makara), highest at 28 degrees. Mars finds disciplined, productive expression in the earth sign of Saturn.

Debilitation: Cancer (Karka), lowest at 28 degrees. Mars is extremely uncomfortable in the emotional, watery, protective sign of Cancer.

Moolatrikona: Aries (0-12 degrees).

Periods: Mars rules the Vimsottari dasa of 7 years. Tuesdays (Mangalvar) belong to Mars.

CHAPTER 4 — MERCURY (BUDHA)

Nature: Mercury is a mixed planet — Rajasic-Tamasic — capable of being influenced strongly by the company it keeps. Classical texts state that Mercury is a prince among planets (Yuvaraja), rational and intellectual, but easily colored by the planets with which it associates. A Mercury with benefics becomes highly benefic; a Mercury with malefics can become malefic.

Primary Significations: Mercury signifies intelligence (buddhi), intellect, communication, commerce, trade, mathematics, astrology, writing, languages, skills (especially fine motor skills), the discriminating faculty (viveka), and all cerebral activities. Mercury is the karaka of the 3rd house (communication, dexterity) and an important contributor to the 6th house (analytical skills) and the overall chart's intelligence indicators.

Body Parts Ruled: Mercury rules the nervous system, the skin, the arms and shoulders, the tongue, the thyroid (shared with other planets), and all organs related to communication and fine motor activity. Diseases include nervous disorders, skin conditions, speech impediments, thyroid imbalances, anxiety, and neurological issues.

Professions: Writing, journalism, teaching, accounting, mathematics, astrology, trade and commerce, brokerage, advocacy, translating, software development, and all intellectually skilled professions.

Relatives: Mercury signifies the maternal uncle (mama) and the younger siblings in some classical systems. Mercury also relates to all persons of similar age to the native — friends, colleagues, and companions.

Substances and Materials: Emerald, green cloth, books, paper, pen, mixed metals (especially alloys), and all things related to the written word and calculation.

Color: Green (the emerald green associated with Mercury).

Directional Strength: Mercury gains Dig Bala in the 1st house (lagna) — the ascendant — where intellectual self-expression is most powerful.

Natural Friends: The Sun and Venus are natural friends of Mercury. Mercury has no natural enemies (similar to the Moon). It is neutral toward Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn.

Own Signs: Mercury rules Gemini (Mithuna) and Virgo (Kanya). Gemini expresses the communicative, dual, social side of Mercury; Virgo expresses the analytical, practical, service-oriented side.

Exaltation: Virgo (Kanya), highest at 15 degrees. Mercury's exaltation in its own analytical sign reflects the perfection of discriminating intelligence.

Debilitation: Pisces (Meena), lowest at 15 degrees. Mercury's debilitation in the sign of dissolution and intuition reflects the dissolution of rational discrimination by oceanic, undifferentiated consciousness.

Moolatrikona: Virgo (16-20 degrees), within the broader Virgo placement.

Periods: Mercury rules the Vimsottari dasa of 17 years. Wednesdays (Budhavar) belong to Mercury.

CHAPTER 5 — JUPITER (BRIHASPATI / GURU)

Nature: Jupiter is Sattvic in quality — the most purely sattvic of all planets. Jupiter is Guru (preceptor), the divine teacher of the gods (Devaguru). Jupiter represents wisdom (jnana), dharma (righteousness), devotion (bhakti), and the expansion of consciousness.

Primary Significations: Jupiter signifies wisdom, knowledge, philosophy, dharma, religion, spirituality, the Guru or teacher, husband (for female charts — Pati karaka), children (especially sons — Putra karaka), wealth (Dhana karaka), fat, yellow substances, the northeast direction, and all matters related to expansion, generosity, and the higher mind. Jupiter is the most important significator: karaka of the 2nd house (wealth, family), 5th house (children, intellect), and 9th house (dharma, guru, fortune).

Body Parts Ruled: Jupiter rules the liver, the hips and thighs, fat tissue (medha dhatu), the arterial system, and the overall sense of health and well-being. Diseases include liver disorders, obesity, diabetes (in excess), thigh injuries, and all conditions related to overindulgence and expansion.

Professions: Teaching, law, judiciary, religion, spirituality, finance, banking, advisory roles, government ministers, philosophy, and all professions associated with wisdom and ethical governance.

Relatives: Jupiter signifies the husband in female charts, children (especially sons), the Guru or spiritual preceptor, the paternal grandfather, and all wise, learned figures.

Substances and Materials: Gold (shared with the Sun), yellow sapphire, yellow cloth, turmeric, sacred texts, and all things associated with spiritual teaching and prosperity.

Color: Yellow (the golden yellow of turmeric, saffron, and gold).

Directional Strength: Jupiter gains Dig Bala in the 1st house (lagna), where wisdom and dharmic self-expression are most fully embodied.

Natural Friends: The Sun, Moon, and Mars are natural friends of Jupiter. Jupiter is enmical to Mercury and Venus. It is neutral toward Saturn.

Own Signs: Jupiter rules Sagittarius (Dhanus) and Pisces (Meena). Sagittarius expresses Jupiter's outward-moving, dharmic, philosophical, adventurous qualities. Pisces expresses Jupiter's inward-turning, compassionate, dissolving, spiritual qualities.

Exaltation: Cancer (Karka), highest at 5 degrees. Jupiter finds its greatest compassionate, nurturing, and expansive expression in the Moon's watery, emotional sign.

Debilitation: Capricorn (Makara), lowest at 5 degrees. Jupiter's expansive wisdom is constrained by Saturn's earthy practicality and ambition.

Moolatrikona: Sagittarius (0-10 degrees).

Periods: Jupiter rules the Vimsottari dasa of 16 years. Thursdays (Guruvara/Brihaspativara) belong to Jupiter.

CHAPTER 6 — VENUS (SHUKRA)

Nature: Venus is Rajasic in quality — the planet of desire (kama), pleasure, and the refinement of sensory experience. Venus is Shukracharya, the preceptor of the demons (Asura Guru), but in the context of jyotish, Venus represents the highest refinement of earthly beauty and the art of living.

Primary Significations: Venus signifies beauty (saundarya), love, romance, marriage, sexual pleasure, artistic creativity, luxury, comfort, vehicles, gems, music, dance, poetry, perfumes, flowers, and all that is beautiful and pleasurable in the material world. Venus is the most important significator: Karaka of the 7th house (marriage, partnerships) and the 4th house (comforts, vehicles). For male charts, Venus is the Kalatrakaraka (significator of the wife).

Body Parts Ruled: Venus rules the reproductive organs (female), the urinary tract, the kidneys, the eyes (especially the visual beauty), the throat, and the hormonal system. Diseases include venereal diseases, urinary tract infections, kidney disorders, hormonal imbalances, and disorders of the reproductive system in women.

Professions: Arts, music, dance, poetry, fashion, beauty services, luxury goods, hospitality, diplomacy, entertainment, and all aesthetically refined professions.

Relatives: Venus signifies the wife (in male charts), all female relatives, and partners in general.

Substances and Materials: Diamond, white sapphire, white cloth, silver (shared with Moon), flowers, perfumes, music instruments, sandalwood, and all beautiful or aromatic substances.

Color: White (pure white or cream) and multicolored (Venus is associated with the full spectrum of visual beauty).

Directional Strength: Venus gains Dig Bala in the 4th house — the nadir — where sensory comfort, domestic beauty, and pleasure are centered.

Natural Friends: Mercury and Saturn are natural friends of Venus. Venus is enmical to the Sun and Moon. It is neutral toward Mars and Jupiter.

Own Signs: Venus rules Taurus (Vrishabha) and Libra (Tula). Taurus expresses the earthy, sensual, material side of Venus. Libra expresses the social, balanced, partnership-oriented side.

Exaltation: Pisces (Meena), highest at 27 degrees. Venus finds its most compassionate, spiritually devoted, and artistically inspired expression in the sign of dissolution and divine love.

Debilitation: Virgo (Kanya), lowest at 27 degrees. Venus's romantic, aesthetic nature is constrained by Virgo's critical, analytical, service-oriented energy.

Moolatrikona: Libra (0-15 degrees).

Periods: Venus rules the Vimsottari dasa of 20 years — the longest dasa period among the classical planets. Fridays (Shukravar) belong to Venus.

CHAPTER 7 — SATURN (SHANI)

Nature: Saturn is Tamasic in quality — the planet of darkness, delay, restriction, karma, old age, disease, death, and the slow grinding of time. Saturn is the great teacher through adversity, ensuring that karma is worked out thoroughly and completely. In the Brihat Jataka tradition, Saturn is described as having a large body, brownish teeth, being lean and stiff-limbed, with yellowish and brown eyes.

Primary Significations: Saturn signifies longevity (ayu), sorrows, servant class, laborers, old age, death, chronic disease, forests, dark places, discipline, endurance, delay, restriction, poverty when afflicted, mines, iron and steel, oil, coal, the masses, democracy, and all things related to time, karma, and the lower strata of society. Saturn is the karaka of the 8th house (longevity, hidden matters) and the 12th house (loss, liberation, isolation).

Body Parts Ruled: Saturn rules the bones, teeth, nails, knees, joints, the nervous system (in a degenerative sense), the muscles, the spleen, and all organs related to structure, rigidity, and the mineral content of the body. Diseases include arthritis, osteoporosis, chronic conditions of all types, nervous degeneration, depression, dental problems, and all slow-developing wasting conditions.

Professions: Mining, engineering (heavy construction), oil and gas, iron and steel industry, real estate (land), agriculture (subsistence farming), government service in lower departments, judiciary, social work, and all professions involving the management of masses of people or long, sustained effort.

Relatives: Saturn signifies servants, lower-class persons, the paternal uncle (in some classical assignments), and all elderly or sickly persons.

Substances and Materials: Iron, steel, lead, coal, oil, sesame, black cloth, sapphire (blue), and all dark, heavy, or old substances.

Color: Dark blue, black, or dark brown. Saturn's colors are the colors of darkness, depth, and heavy metal.

Directional Strength: Saturn gains Dig Bala in the 7th house — the house of the setting sun — representing the descending, darkening phase of the day.

Natural Friends: Mercury and Venus are natural friends of Saturn. Saturn is enmical to the Sun and Moon, and neutral toward Mars and Jupiter.

Own Signs: Saturn rules Capricorn (Makara) and Aquarius (Kumbha). Capricorn expresses Saturn's practical, ambitious, worldly achievement. Aquarius expresses Saturn's humanitarian, progressive, systematic social qualities.

Exaltation: Libra (Tula), highest at 20 degrees. Saturn finds its most just, balanced, and socially responsible expression in Venus's sign of equity and partnership.

Debilitation: Aries (Mesha), lowest at 20 degrees. Saturn's slow, careful energy is disrupted by Aries's impulsive, aggressive, individualistic fire.

Moolatrikona: Aquarius (0-20 degrees).

Periods: Saturn rules the Vimsottari dasa of 19 years. Saturdays (Shanivara) belong to Saturn.

CHAPTER 8 — RAHU (THE NORTH NODE)

Nature: Rahu is Tamasic in quality — smoky, shadowy, illusory, and associated with the realm of maya (illusion) and worldly desire. Rahu is the ascending lunar node — the point where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic going northward. Rahu is likened to Saturn in its effects in many classical texts (Shani-Sadrishya).

Primary Significations: Rahu signifies foreign lands and foreign persons, outcastes, unconventional behavior, sudden events, obsessive desires, technology, innovation, epidemics, poisons, smokiness, ambiguity, confusion, and all that is foreign, unusual, or transgressive of social norms. Rahu is associated with worldly maya, excessive worldly desire, and the amplification of whatever sign and house it occupies.

Body Parts Ruled: Rahu rules the nervous system (in conjunction with Mercury and Saturn), the skin (in conjunction with Saturn), and is associated with unusual or mysterious diseases. Rahu-related illnesses are often difficult to diagnose — mysterious, chronic, or multi-symptomatic. Cancer, drug addiction, and psychiatric conditions are associated with Rahu.

Professions: Technology (especially cutting-edge innovation), foreign trade, aviation, film, photography, chemicals, political strategy, espionage, and all professions involving deception, technology, or foreign interaction.

Color: Smoky, dark blue, or mottled. Rahu's color is the color of smoke and shadow.

Directional Strength: Rahu is considered strong in the 6th house (overcoming enemies through unconventional means), the 10th house (career advancement through unusual paths), and the 3rd house (communication with foreign or unusual content).

Natural Friends: Rahu is generally friendly toward Saturn, Mercury, and Venus. It is enmical to the Sun and Moon. Relationships vary by classical authority.

Exaltation: There is classical debate — some texts give Taurus (Vrishabha) as Rahu's exaltation, others give Gemini (Mithuna) or Virgo. The most commonly cited positions are Taurus for exaltation and Scorpio for debilitation, or alternatively Gemini for exaltation and Sagittarius for debilitation.

Periods: Rahu rules the Vimsottari dasa of 18 years.

CHAPTER 9 — KETU (THE SOUTH NODE)

Nature: Ketu is Tamasic in quality — intensely spiritual, moksha-oriented, and related to sudden separations, liberation, and past-life karmic completion. Ketu is the descending lunar node — the point where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic going southward. Ketu is likened to Mars in its effects (Mangala-Sadrishya).

Primary Significations: Ketu signifies liberation (moksha), past-life experiences, spiritual insight, psychic abilities, renunciation, detachment, losses, wounds, flags, multi-colored objects, dogs, and all sharp, pointed things. Ketu is associated with spiritual realization, occult powers (siddhis), sudden separations, and the dissolution of worldly attachments.

Body Parts Ruled: Ketu rules the feet (in tandem with Pisces), wounds and injuries from pointed objects, the anus, the tail end of various bodily processes, and is associated with mysterious fever-like conditions. Diseases include accidents involving pointed objects, mysterious fevers, and spiritual-related health crises.

Professions: Spirituality, occultism, astrology, healing arts (especially alternative or spiritual healing), military (special forces), and all professions involving extreme focus, sacrifice, or specialized expertise from past-life mastery.

Color: Multi-colored, variegated, or smoky reddish-gray. Ketu's colors are mixed and changeable.

Directional Strength: Ketu is considered strong in the 12th house (liberation, dissolution) and the 3rd house (courage, past-life abilities in communication). Like Rahu, its directional strength is not as classically codified as the seven traditional planets.

Natural Friends: Ketu is generally considered friendly toward Mars, Venus, and Saturn. It is hostile toward the Sun and Moon in most classical formulations.

Exaltation: The complement of Rahu's position — if Rahu is exalted in Taurus, Ketu is exalted in Scorpio. If Rahu's exaltation is Gemini, Ketu's is Sagittarius.

Periods: Ketu rules the Vimsottari dasa of 7 years.

PART TWO: HOUSE SIGNIFICATIONS AND BHAVA KARAKA

CHAPTER 10 — THE TWELVE HOUSES AND THEIR NATURAL SIGNIFICATORS

1st House (Lagna / Tanu Bhava): The first house governs the body (tanu), self, health, vitality, appearance, character, and the overall tone of the chart. Its natural significator is the Sun (for self) and Mars (for vitality). The prosperity of the 1st house determines the overall strength of the native. When the lagna lord is strong and well-placed, the entire chart is energized. Malefics in the lagna without benefic aspects create health challenges and difficult early life. Benefics in or aspecting the lagna protect the native's health and character.

2nd House (Dhana Bhava): The second house governs accumulated wealth (dhana), family (kutumba), speech (vak), face and right eye, food, early education, and all stored assets. Its natural significator is Jupiter (for wealth and family). A strong 2nd lord and a strong Jupiter give financial prosperity and family harmony. Malefics in the 2nd can give harsh or dishonest speech. The 2nd house is also considered a maraka (death-inflicting) house — its lord can trigger death in its dasa when life span is exhausted.

3rd House (Bhratri / Sahaja Bhava): The third house governs younger siblings, courage, short journeys, communication, hands and arms, writing, mental dexterity, and servants. Its natural significator is Mars (for younger brothers) and Mercury (for communication and skill). A strong 3rd lord and Mars give courageous, supportive siblings and professional initiative. Malefics in the 3rd are not always harmful — Mars here often gives exceptional courage and the ability to overcome enemies.

4th House (Sukha / Matri Bhava): The fourth house governs the mother, home, land, property, vehicles, education, inner happiness, ancestral heritage, and the chest. Its natural significator is the Moon (for mother and emotional contentment) and Mercury (for education). A strong 4th lord, Moon, and Mercury give maternal happiness, good education, property, and emotional stability. Malefics in the 4th create domestic troubles and difficulties with the mother. The 4th house also governs the end of the career (the grave or retirement — the nadir).

5th House (Putra / Suta Bhava): The fifth house governs children (especially sons), intelligence (buddhi), creative expression, past merit (purva punya), speculation, mantras, spiritual practices, and romantic relationships (in some classical systems). Its natural significator is Jupiter. A strong 5th lord and Jupiter give intelligent children, brilliant intellect, and spiritual merit. Malefics in the 5th can reduce children or create difficulties in childbearing.

6th House (Shatru / Roga Bhava): The sixth house governs enemies, diseases, debts, litigation, service, maternal uncle, difficulties, and competitive situations. Its natural significator is Mars (for enemies and vitality in competition) and Saturn (for chronic disease and service). Malefics in the 6th can give serious health challenges or powerful enemies, but also the ability to overcome adversity. Benefics in the 6th may reduce the native's ability to fight enemies (being too accommodating).

7th House (Kalatra / Jaya Bhava): The seventh house governs the spouse, marriage, business partnerships, foreign travel, sexual relationships, the lower abdomen, and open enemies (as opposed to the hidden enemies of the 12th). Its natural significator is Venus (for the wife and marriage). A strong 7th lord and Venus give a happy, prosperous marriage. Malefics in the 7th create marital difficulties, delayed marriage, or problematic partnerships. The 7th is also a maraka house — its lord can trigger death in its dasa.

8th House (Ayus / Mrityu Bhava): The eighth house governs longevity (ayu), sudden events, transformations, death, inheritance, occult knowledge, chronic disease, research, hidden matters, and the genitals. Its natural significator is Saturn (for longevity and chronic conditions). A strong 8th lord and Saturn can give exceptional longevity. The 8th house is one of the most complex houses — it is both the house of death and the house of transformation and regeneration. Benefics in the 8th contribute to longevity.

9th House (Bhagya / Dharma Bhava): The ninth house governs fortune (bhagya), dharma, higher learning, the father, the Guru, pilgrimages, long journeys, philosophy, law, religion, and divine grace. Its natural significator is Jupiter and the Sun (for the father). A strong 9th lord and Jupiter give exceptional fortune, a fortunate father, and a dharmic life. The 9th house is considered the most auspicious trikona (trine) house and its activation in dasa periods often brings significant fortune.

10th House (Karma / Rajya Bhava): The tenth house governs profession (karma), status, authority, the father's lineage, public reputation, actions, the knee, and all activity in the external world. Its natural significator is Saturn (for hard work and discipline), the Sun (for authority), Jupiter (for wisdom in action), and Mercury (for skills applied in career). A strong 10th lord and benefic planets in the 10th give professional success, status, and recognition. This is the most active house in the chart — where destiny is enacted in the external world.

11th House (Labha / Aya Bhava): The eleventh house governs gains (labha), income, elder siblings, friends, aspirations, left ear, left calf, and the fulfillment of desires. Its natural significator is Jupiter (for gains and abundance) and Saturn (for systematic income). Malefics in the 11th are generally not harmful — Saturn here gives disciplined, steady gains. A strong 11th lord gives financial prosperity and social connections. This house activates the native's ability to receive the fruits of their efforts.

12th House (Vyaya / Moksha Bhava): The twelfth house governs losses, expenditure, isolation, foreign lands, imprisonment, hospitalization, sleep, liberation (moksha), the left eye, and all matters related to the dissolution of worldly ties. Its natural significator is Saturn (for isolation, renunciation) and Ketu (for liberation). Benefics in the 12th give expenditure for good causes and spiritual liberation. Malefics in the 12th can give imprisonment, serious health challenges, or financial drain.

PART THREE: NABHASA YOGAS — PLANETARY DISTRIBUTION YOGAS

CHAPTER 11 — AKRITI YOGAS (SHAPE YOGAS)

The Nabhasa Yogas (Heavenly Yogas) are formed based on the distribution of planets among signs and houses. Brihat Jataka describes these systematically. They are divided into Akriti (shape) yogas, Sankhya (number) yogas, and Ashraya (support) yogas.

Rajju Yoga: All seven classical planets occupy moveable (chara) signs — Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn. The native is always wandering, fond of travel, and earns through journeys. The native may not settle in the birthplace and derives livelihood from foreign countries. Travel is not by choice but by necessity, and the native is constantly on the move in career and personal life. Rajju (a rope) suggests the native is tied to perpetual motion.

Musala Yoga: All seven classical planets occupy fixed (sthira) signs — Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius. The native is obstinate, determined, and resistant to change. This yoga gives stability of character, steadiness in purpose, fame for courage and wealth, and honor from kings or persons in authority. The native is a pillar of the community and does not bend easily to external pressure. Life is stable and settled.

Nala Yoga: All seven classical planets occupy dual (dwiswabhava) signs — Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces. The native is skilled in many arts, engaged in numerous activities simultaneously, and has an uneven or complex body form. The native is industrious and clever in various crafts. Career involves multiple simultaneous activities and the native adapts to changing circumstances with remarkable facility.

Mala Yoga (Garland Yoga): Benefics (Jupiter, Venus, Mercury, and full Moon) occupy the 1st, 4th, 7th, and 10th houses (the four kendras). The native wears a garland of planetary blessings at the most important angles of the chart. This yoga gives royalty-like status, wealth, beauty, enjoyment of sensual pleasures, access to the best of material comforts, and long life. The native is universally respected and lives a life of considerable enjoyment and material abundance.

Sarpa Yoga (Serpent Yoga): Malefics (Sun, Mars, Saturn, and the nodes in affliction) occupy all the kendras. This yoga is considered highly inauspicious, producing a life of suffering, poverty, meanness, and sinful conduct. The native may be deceptive, cruel, or given to harmful activities. Classical texts describe the Sarpa Yoga native as one who lives in misery, is ungrateful, base, and may be born into poverty or destitution.

Gada Yoga: Planets occupy the 1st and 4th houses or the 4th and 7th houses or the 7th and 10th houses. The native born in Gada yoga is skilled in performing rituals, devoted to dharmic activities, wealthy, endowed with a good mind, and famous. Career may involve performing arts, religious ceremonies, or activities that serve community wellbeing.

Sanaha Yoga: Planets occupy the 2nd and 8th houses from each other (opposition pattern). The native is skilled in various activities, determined and persistent, fond of accumulating wealth, and courageous in the face of opposition. The native wins through endurance and is not easily defeated by enemies.

Vibhuka (or Gola) Yoga: All planets are in one sign. This rare configuration gives a concentrated, one-pointed energy. The native is exceptional in one particular field but may lack balance across other life areas.

Srikantha Yoga: Planets in the 1st, 5th, and 9th houses (the dharma trine). The native is virtuous, fortunate, devotionally inclined, and marked by dharmic merit. Life is characterized by fortune, learning, and spiritual activity.

Hala Yoga (Plough Yoga): Planets in the 2nd, 6th, and 10th houses (the artha trine). The native is skilled in agriculture, trade, and practical economic endeavors. The native earns through sustained, systematic effort and is devoted to material productivity.

Vajra Yoga (Thunderbolt Yoga): Benefics in the 1st and 7th houses, malefics in the 4th and 10th — or vice versa. The native is devoted to scholarly and righteous activities in the first half of life, and happy, wealthy, and prosperous in the second half. This yoga is associated with dramatic changes between the two halves of life.

Yava Yoga (Barley Yoga): Benefics in the 1st, 4th, 7th, and 10th houses and malefics in the 2nd and 12th — or the reverse arrangement. The native born in Yava yoga enjoys happiness, learning, and spiritual virtue in the first and last portions of life while experiencing difficulties in the middle portions.

Kamala Yoga: All planets in the four kendras — 1st, 4th, 7th, and 10th houses. The native is like the lotus (kamala) rising from the mud to bloom — virtuous, wealthy, famous, and long-lived. This yoga gives extraordinary strength to the chart due to the concentration of all planetary energy at the most powerful angles.

Vapi Yoga (Well Yoga): Planets occupy the 3rd, 6th, 9th, and 12th houses (the dusthanas and their complements). The native accumulates and preserves wealth carefully, like water collected in a well. The native may be somewhat secretive about wealth but accumulates it systematically.

Yupa Yoga (Sacrificial Post Yoga): Planets in the first four consecutive houses (1st through 4th). The native is devoted to religious sacrifice, learning, and contemplative activities. Career involves religious or scholarly pursuits.

Ishu Yoga (Arrow Yoga): Planets in the middle four consecutive houses (4th through 7th). The native is skilled, crafty, and determined in professional activities. Career involves skilled craftsmanship or technical work.

Shakti Yoga (Spear Yoga): Planets in the last four consecutive houses (7th through 10th). The native struggles with difficulties but overcomes them through exceptional willpower and strength of character.

Danda Yoga (Staff Yoga): Planets in three consecutive houses. This yoga gives the native a one-pointed focus in life, though the specific effect depends on which three houses are occupied.

CHAPTER 12 — CHANDRA YOGAS (LUNAR YOGAS)

Sunapha Yoga: A planet other than the Sun occupies the 2nd house from the Moon. The native born in Sunapha yoga is intelligent, wealthy, a king or equal to a king, skilled in various arts, and praised by all. This yoga activates the Moon's creative and intelligent energies through the support of a planet in the 2nd from the Moon.

Anapha Yoga: A planet other than the Sun occupies the 12th house from the Moon. The native born in Anapha yoga is healthy, pleasant in appearance, disciplined in personal habits, virtuous, charitable, and respected. This yoga activates the Moon's expressive potential through a planet providing resources before the Moon's position.

Durudhara Yoga: Planets other than the Sun occupy both the 2nd and 12th houses from the Moon (both Sunapha and Anapha conditions satisfied simultaneously). The native born in Durudhara yoga is wealthy, happy, generous with gifts and conveyances, and endowed with a retinue of attendants. The Moon is doubly supported — from both sides — creating strong emotional and material support.

Kemadruma Yoga: No planet occupies the 2nd or 12th house from the Moon. This is considered one of the most challenging yogas. The native born in Kemadruma yoga may suffer poverty, mental disturbance, servitude, and social disgrace. The Moon is isolated, with no planetary support from either side, leading to emotional and material isolation. However, classical texts note important exceptions: Kemadruma is cancelled if a planet occupies a kendra from the Moon or from the lagna, or if the Moon is associated with a planet.

CHAPTER 13 — LUNAR TITHI YOGAS

Classical Brihat Jataka describes combinations of the Moon's tithi (lunar day), vara (weekday), and nakshatra (lunar mansion) as creating specific planetary yogas that modify the basic chart results:

The five lunar sidereal periods create combinations called Nitya Yogas, calculated as the combined longitude of the Sun and Moon divided by 13°20'. These 27 Nitya Yogas range from the most auspicious (Vishkambha begins the cycle) through various qualities to the most challenging. Birth in auspicious Nitya Yogas adds fortune and good character; birth in inauspicious ones creates obstacles that must be overcome.

Key Tithi Combinations: Birth on Shukla Paksha Purnima (Full Moon) with a strong Moon in a favorable sign is considered exceptionally auspicious — the native has excellent emotional constitution, public appeal, and natural magnetic charm. Birth on Krishna Paksha Amavasya (New Moon) with the Moon close to the Sun indicates the native must work harder to develop emotional independence and clarity. Birth on specific tithis ruled by auspicious planets (1, 5, 7, 11, 15 in Shukla Paksha) generally confers good fortune.

PART FOUR: BALARISHTA — EARLY LIFE AFFLICTIONS AND CHILDHOOD MORTALITY

CHAPTER 14 — INDICATORS OF CHILDHOOD DANGER

Varahamihira devotes considerable attention to Balarishta — the afflictions that can cause childhood mortality or severe health crises in the early years of life. Understanding Balarishta is essential for the traditional purpose of astrology: advising families on the welfare of newborns.

Primary Balarishta Indicators:
1. The Moon in the 6th, 8th, or 12th house, heavily afflicted by malefics (Sun, Mars, or Saturn) without benefic aspects or conjunctions. The Moon represents life-force (prana) in infancy — when afflicted in dusthana houses, it indicates vulnerability in the early years.

2. The lagna and the Moon both occupying the same or opposite navamsas as Saturn or Mars, without benefic protection. The navamsha is particularly important in Balarishta analysis.

3. Malefics in all kendras — Sarpa Yoga formation — creates Balarishta when no benefics aspect the kendras.

4. The lagna lord in the 8th house and the 8th lord in the lagna, particularly when both are malefics or afflicted.

5. The Sun and Moon in the 6th, 8th, or 12th houses simultaneously, creating dual malefic pressure on the two main vitality indicators.

6. Birth during a malefic solar or lunar eclipse, with the eclipse degree coinciding with the birth lagna or Moon.

7. The Moon in the last degrees of a sign (sandhi) or in the 8th navamsha of any sign, afflicted by malefics.

Protection Against Balarishta:
1. Benefics (Jupiter, Venus, or well-placed Mercury) aspecting or conjoining the Moon cancel or mitigate Balarishta significantly.

2. Jupiter in a kendra from the lagna provides the strongest protection — the great benefic at an angle shelters the native from early-life threats.

3. The Moon in its own sign (Cancer) or exaltation (Taurus), even if in a dusthana, is stronger and more resistant to Balarishta.

4. Venus and Mercury in the lagna or aspecting it create a protective shield for the native.

5. Multiple benefics in the 5th or 9th house provide strong karmic protection.

6. The strength and condition of the lagna lord is ultimately the most important factor — if the lagna lord is strong, the native overcomes Balarishta.

Varahamihira also notes that even when Balarishta indicators are present, if powerful protective yogas are simultaneously formed, the native survives the early-life crisis and may in fact be strengthened by it — the experience of early-life challenge building the resilience that will later fuel great achievement.

PART FIVE: AYURDAYA — LONGEVITY DETERMINATION

CHAPTER 15 — METHODS OF CALCULATING LIFESPAN

Varahamihira presents three classical methods for calculating longevity:

PINDAYU METHOD: In the Pindayu method, each planet is assigned a specific maximum number of years it can contribute to longevity based on its position in the chart. These raw years are then modified by the planet's strength (full strength planets contribute their maximum; diminished strength planets contribute proportionally reduced years). The total sum of all planetary contributions gives the native's longevity.

Maximum years by planet in the Pindayu system:
Sun: 19 years maximum
Moon: 25 years maximum
Mars: 15 years maximum
Mercury: 12 years maximum
Jupiter: 15 years maximum
Venus: 21 years maximum
Saturn: 20 years maximum

A planet contributes its full years when: exalted, in its own sign, in moolatrikona, or in a kendra. It contributes half years when in the 2nd, 3rd, 5th, 9th, or 11th houses. It contributes one-third years when in the 6th, 8th, or 12th houses. Additionally, the Pindayu years must be reduced by one-third (eka-trikona) for planets in hayana or ayana that are adverse to the planet's nature.

NISARGAYU METHOD: The Nisargayu method assigns natural lifespans to the three categories of life: Alpayu (short life — up to 32 years), Madhyayu (medium life — 33 to 64 years), and Purnayu (full life — 65 to 120 years, with extreme longevity possible for highly powerful charts). The category is determined by assessing the overall strength of the 8th house and its lord, the longevity-karaka Saturn, and the primary life-force indicators — the lagna, lagna lord, and the Sun and Moon.

Signs of Alpayu (Short Life):
- Lagna and lagna lord both heavily afflicted by malefics
- The Moon in the 8th house, afflicted and without benefic support
- The 8th lord in the lagna, aspected by malefics
- Multiple Balarishta indicators present
- Saturn in the 1st house heavily afflicted
- Maraka lords (2nd and 7th house lords) activated early in the dasa sequence

Signs of Madhyayu (Medium Life):
- Mixed indicators: some afflictions to longevity houses but also some protective factors
- Lagna lord moderately strong, some benefic protection to Moon
- Saturn reasonably placed, 8th house neither severely afflicted nor strongly protected
- Marakas activated in middle dasa periods

Signs of Purnayu (Full Life):
- Lagna lord and the Sun and Moon strongly placed
- Jupiter in a kendra, strongly disposed
- Saturn strong in its own sign or exaltation
- 8th house well protected by benefic aspect or benefic 8th lord
- Benefics in all four kendras (Mala Yoga)
- Powerful Ayurdaya yoga like Saturn in its own sign in the 8th

AMSAYU METHOD: The Amsayu method calculates longevity from the navamsha positions of planets. Each navamsha position contributes a specific number of years. This method is used as a secondary check on the primary Pindayu or Nisargayu calculation.

CHAPTER 16 — MARAKA PLANETS AND DEATH TIMING

Maraka planets (death-inflicting planets) are the lords of the 2nd and 7th houses from the lagna, and planets associated with or aspecting these lords. Classical doctrine on marakas:

1. The lord of the 2nd house and the lord of the 7th house are primary marakas. Their dasas and antardasas can trigger death or severe health crises when the native's allotted lifespan (ayurdaya) has been completed.

2. Planets associated with the 2nd and 7th lords become secondary marakas. A planet in the 2nd or 7th house is also maraka by position.

3. Saturn, as the significator of death and longevity, always has a special role as the ultimate arbiter of the lifespan. Saturn's dasa or antardasa, when activated at the end of the allotted lifespan, is a classical trigger for death.

4. Malefics in the 6th, 8th, and 12th houses (the dusthanas or houses of suffering) can act as maraka agents when they are lords of maraka houses or associate with maraka lords.

5. Benefics generally do not act as marakas — Jupiter, Venus, and Mercury in their own dasas tend to preserve and protect life rather than end it. However, if a benefic planet is simultaneously the lord of the 2nd or 7th house, it can become a maraka in those specific house-lord roles.

6. The 3rd and 8th houses are trikona from each other in a specific classification — they form the Ayus (lifespan) pair. When malefics occupy both the 3rd and 8th simultaneously, longevity is threatened.

PART SIX: DASA PHALA — RESULTS OF PLANETARY PERIODS

CHAPTER 17 — VIMSHOTTARI DASA EFFECTS

Vimshottari Dasa is the primary timing system of Jyotish. The total cycle covers 120 years, distributed among the nine grahas based on the Moon's nakshatra at birth:

Ketu: 7 years
Venus: 20 years
Sun: 6 years
Moon: 10 years
Mars: 7 years
Rahu: 18 years
Jupiter: 16 years
Saturn: 19 years
Mercury: 17 years

SUN DASA (6 YEARS): The Sun dasa generally brings the native into contact with authority, government, and the father's realm. If the natal Sun is strong and well-placed, this period brings recognition from the government, rise in status, and professional promotion. Career in administration or governmental service flourishes. The father's condition, for better or worse, is highlighted during this period. Health concerns may involve the heart, eyes, and bones — particularly if the natal Sun is afflicted. Ego-assertion may create conflicts with authorities if the Sun is placed in a challenging sign. If the Sun is exalted, the dasa can bring extraordinary honors and governmental recognition. If the Sun is debilitated without Neecha Bhanga, the period may bring difficulties with authority figures, health challenges, and loss of position.

MOON DASA (10 YEARS): The Moon dasa brings emotional events, matters related to the mother, home, public dealings, and prosperity or challenges depending on the natal Moon's strength. A full, strong Moon in a favorable sign during this dasa can give prosperity through public dealings, real estate, food business, and maternal blessings. The native's popularity with the masses increases. Family life and domestic happiness are prominent. Emotional sensitivity is heightened and the native may be more susceptible to environmental influence. If the Moon is afflicted (especially in the 6th, 8th, or 12th house, or debilitated in Scorpio), the dasa can bring mental disturbances, emotional crises, difficulties with the mother, and health challenges related to the stomach, blood, and mind.

MARS DASA (7 YEARS): The Mars dasa brings energy, initiative, competition, and Martian themes to the forefront. If natal Mars is strong (in own sign, exaltation, or in a favorable house), this dasa gives exceptional vitality, professional ambition, property acquisition, and success in competitive fields. Courage is high and the native may take bold initiatives that advance career. Younger siblings may feature prominently. If Mars is afflicted — debilitated, in an enemy's sign, or in the 6th, 8th, or 12th without compensating factors — the dasa can bring accidents, conflicts, surgical interventions, property disputes, and health challenges involving blood, muscles, and inflammatory conditions.

RAHU DASA (18 YEARS): The Rahu dasa is one of the most transformative periods in the Vimshottari cycle. Lasting 18 years, it brings dramatic shifts, foreign connections, unconventional career paths, and obsessive engagements with whatever Rahu's natal position signifies. If Rahu is placed favorably (in kendras or in beneficial signs with good aspects), the dasa can bring sudden rise, material prosperity, foreign travel and career advancement, technological success, and fame — though often of an unconventional or controversial nature. If Rahu is poorly placed or afflicted, the dasa can bring confusion, deception, health challenges involving mysterious conditions, foreign misadventures, and karmic upheavals. The beginning and end of Rahu dasa are often the most turbulent portions.

JUPITER DASA (16 YEARS): The Jupiter dasa is generally the most auspicious dasa for most natives, as Jupiter is the great benefic. If natal Jupiter is strong, this 16-year period can bring wealth, children, wisdom, spiritual advancement, career recognition, and marriage (if not yet married). The native expands in all dharmic directions — through education, family, social standing, and inner wisdom. Religious and philosophical pursuits flourish. If Jupiter is debilitated (in Capricorn) without Neecha Bhanga, the dasa may be less benefic — financial misjudgments, legal complications, or challenges in relationships with Guru-figures. The native's level of dharmic merit from past lives significantly determines the quality of the Jupiter dasa.

SATURN DASA (19 YEARS): The Saturn dasa, lasting 19 years, is a period of karmic reckoning, disciplined effort, and slow but cumulative achievement. If natal Saturn is strong (exalted in Libra, in own sign Capricorn or Aquarius, or in a favorable house), this dasa can bring exceptional career achievement, social recognition, and wealth through sustained effort — often after initial years of struggle. Real estate, business, and long-term professional projects come to fruition. The native's character is tested and purified. If Saturn is afflicted or in an inimical sign, the dasa may bring chronic health challenges, delays in professional advancement, sorrows related to elderly persons or servants, and feelings of isolation. The middle years of the Saturn dasa are typically the most productive.

MERCURY DASA (17 YEARS): The Mercury dasa activates the native's intellectual and communicative faculties. If natal Mercury is strong (exalted in Virgo or in own sign Gemini/Virgo), this period brings success in writing, trade, teaching, and all intellectual pursuits. Business ventures and commercial dealings prosper. Multiple simultaneous projects advance. Younger siblings may play important roles. If Mercury is debilitated or afflicted, the dasa may bring communication difficulties, commercial setbacks, disputes with siblings, nervous system issues, and challenges in education.

KETU DASA (7 YEARS): The Ketu dasa, though brief at 7 years, can be intensely significant for spiritual evolution. Ketu brings sudden separations, spiritual insights, unexpected losses, and moksha-oriented experiences. If natal Ketu is in an auspicious placement (8th house for moksha, 12th house for liberation, or 5th for spiritual merit), the dasa can bring powerful spiritual realizations, occult abilities, and inner transformation. Events of the Ketu dasa often feel fated and beyond ordinary control — as if one is completing past-life karma rather than creating new karma. If Ketu is poorly placed and afflicted, the dasa may bring losses, mysterious health conditions, accidents, and a general sense of dissolution of worldly supports.

VENUS DASA (20 YEARS): The Venus dasa, the longest in the Vimshottari cycle, governs a period of material prosperity, artistic flowering, and relational abundance — when natal Venus is well placed. Marriage often occurs during Venus dasa or its sub-periods. Arts, music, luxury, comfort, and social pleasures are heightened. Financial prosperity is generally excellent. The native may acquire vehicles, beautiful possessions, and enjoy the best that material life offers. If Venus is debilitated or severely afflicted, the dasa may bring relationship difficulties, health challenges involving the reproductive system or kidneys, and complications related to over-indulgence.

ANTARDASA PRINCIPLES: Within each main dasa, the sub-periods (antardasas) of all nine planets operate in sequence. The antardasa of a friendly planet within a benefic dasa amplifies the dasa's positive results. The antardasa of an inimical planet within a malefic dasa intensifies difficulties. Cross-combinations — beneficial antardasa within a challenging dasa, or challenging antardasa within a beneficial dasa — produce mixed results. The antardasa lord's natal strength and house position determine the specific events that manifest.

CHAPTER 18 — YOGAKARAKA AND DASA RESULTS

A Yogakaraka planet is one that rules both a kendra (1st, 4th, 7th, 10th) and a trikona (1st, 5th, 9th) simultaneously, making it a doubly benefic planet for that specific ascendant. When a Yogakaraka's dasa period arrives, it typically produces extraordinary results — combining the power of both kendra and trikona house lordship.

Yogakarakas by Ascendant:
- Aries (Mesha) Lagna: Saturn (lord of 10th and 11th — not a yogakaraka by strict definition, but Mars as lagna lord has yogakaraka properties). True Yogakaraka for Aries is Saturn in combination with other factors.
- Taurus (Vrishabha) Lagna: Saturn (lord of 9th and 10th) — excellent Yogakaraka.
- Gemini (Mithuna) Lagna: Venus (lord of 5th and 12th — partially). Saturn (lord of 8th and 9th) for some classical authorities.
- Cancer (Karka) Lagna: Mars (lord of 5th and 10th) — powerful Yogakaraka.
- Leo (Simha) Lagna: Mars (lord of 4th and 9th) — excellent Yogakaraka.
- Virgo (Kanya) Lagna: Venus (lord of 2nd and 9th) and Mercury (lord of 1st and 10th) — dual Yogakarakas.
- Libra (Tula) Lagna: Saturn (lord of 4th and 5th) — exceptional Yogakaraka for Libra ascendant.
- Scorpio (Vrischika) Lagna: Moon (lord of 9th) — an important benefic.
- Sagittarius (Dhanus) Lagna: Sun (lord of 9th) — benefic for fortune.
- Capricorn (Makara) Lagna: Venus (lord of 5th and 10th) — powerful Yogakaraka.
- Aquarius (Kumbha) Lagna: Venus (lord of 4th and 9th) — excellent Yogakaraka.
- Pisces (Meena) Lagna: Mars (lord of 2nd and 9th) — beneficial planet.

When a Yogakaraka's dasa is running, even normally difficult houses are elevated. The native experiences the bhoga (enjoyment) of the entire chart's positive potential. If the Yogakaraka is in the lagna, 5th, or 9th house, the dasa effects are maximally powerful.

PART SEVEN: STREE JATAKA — FEMALE HOROSCOPY

CHAPTER 19 — SPECIAL RULES FOR FEMALE CHARTS

Varahamihira dedicates specific chapters to the analysis of female horoscopes, recognizing that the same principles operate differently when applied to the female native. The Brihat Jataka's Stree Jataka section provides classical rules that have been amplified in subsequent texts.

PRIMARY SIGNIFICATORS IN FEMALE CHARTS:
Moon: For a female native, the Moon is the primary indicator of character, health, emotional life, and general wellbeing. A strong Moon in a female chart gives beauty, emotional stability, family happiness, and social respect. An afflicted Moon creates emotional instability, health challenges related to the reproductive system, and difficulties in family life.

Venus: Venus in a female chart still governs beauty, sensuality, and the arts, but also indicates the quality of the woman's relationships with her husband and her capacity for domestic harmony. A strong Venus gives a woman charm, beauty, artistic talent, and a happy marriage. An afflicted Venus can create challenges in marriage, reproductive health issues, and complications in romantic relationships.

Jupiter: In female horoscopy, Jupiter is the Pati Karaka (significator of the husband). A strong Jupiter in a female chart gives an honorable, learned, and prosperous husband. The position of Jupiter by house and sign, and its aspects, indicate the quality of the husband and the marriage. Jupiter in a kendra in the female chart is particularly auspicious for marriage happiness.

Mars: In female charts, Mars has special significance as Mangala (the auspicious) in relation to its infamous Mangala Dosha. Mars in the 1st, 2nd, 4th, 7th, 8th, or 12th house of the female chart is considered to create Kuja Dosha (Mars affliction in marriage), which classical texts warn can create difficulties in marriage or harm to the husband. This doctrine is one of the most practically applied rules in Vedic astrology for marriage compatibility.

7th House Analysis: In the female chart, the 7th house represents the husband, the marriage, and all matters related to intimate partnership. The 7th lord's strength and placement determine the quality of the husband and the longevity of the marriage. Malefics in the 7th house without benefic protection create marital difficulties.

WIDOWHOOD INDICATORS: Classical texts describe specific combinations (widowhood yogas) that indicate potential widowhood or separation:
1. Saturn in the 7th house, aspecting the 7th lord, with no benefic protection.
2. Mars in the 8th house of a female chart, aspecting the 7th house.
3. The lord of the 7th house debilitated or in the 6th, 8th, or 12th house.
4. The Sun and Saturn both connected to the 7th house or 7th lord.
5. Venus (the natural karaka of marriage) in the 8th house, heavily afflicted.

However, each of these indicators requires the absence of protective factors to be operative. If Jupiter aspects the 7th house or its lord, or if Venus is strong, these widowhood indicators are considerably weakened.

CHASTITY YOGA: Combinations indicating chastity and virtue in a female horoscope:
1. The Moon and Venus in odd signs (Aries, Gemini, Leo, Libra, Sagittarius, Aquarius) make a woman virtuous and devoted.
2. Jupiter aspecting the lagna, Moon, and Venus simultaneously gives a highly moral and disciplined character.
3. The lagna lord in the 9th house and the 9th lord in the lagna creates a highly virtuous and dharmic female native.

RAJAYOGA FOR WOMEN: Female natives with powerful Raja Yogas can achieve exceptional positions of authority and social prominence. The same yogas that give men power give women equivalent prominence in their spheres: Venus-Jupiter conjunction in a kendra, for example, gives a woman great social standing and the blessings of both beauty and wisdom. The Moon exalted in a kendra gives public appeal and social influence. Gajakesari Yoga (Jupiter in kendra from Moon) gives a woman wisdom, social respect, and prosperity.

CHAPTER 20 — MARRIAGE TIMING IN FEMALE CHARTS

The traditional Brihat Jataka approach to timing marriage in female horoscopes involves:

1. The dasa and antardasa of Venus: Venus dasa or Venus antardasa is the most common timing for marriage in female charts. When Venus is well-placed, its period actively brings the circumstances for marriage.

2. The dasa and antardasa of the 7th lord: The lord of the 7th house, when its period is active and the chart is otherwise ripe, triggers marriage.

3. Jupiter's transit: When Jupiter transits the natal Venus, 7th house, or its own natal position, marriage circumstances often arise.

4. The 7th lord's nakshatra dasa: The dasa of the lord of the nakshatra in which the 7th lord is placed can bring marriage.

5. In navamsha analysis, the 7th lord of the navamsha (D-9) and the navamsha lagna lord are examined — when their periods are active in the main chart's timing system, marriage becomes ripe.

CHAPTER 21 — PANCHAMAHAPURUSHA YOGAS

Varahamihira, though primarily in Brihat Jataka and amplified in other texts, establishes the Panchamahapurusha (Five Great Person) Yogas, which occur when a non-luminous planet occupies a kendra in its own sign or exaltation sign:

Ruchaka Yoga: Mars in a kendra in Aries, Scorpio (own signs), or Capricorn (exaltation). The native is a great warrior, commander, king-like, physically powerful, courageous, and renowned for military and physical prowess. The native commands armies and is victorious in competition. Body characteristics include a prominent neck, impressive physique, reddish complexion, and Martian bearing.

Bhadra Yoga: Mercury in a kendra in Gemini or Virgo (own signs) or in Virgo exaltation. The native is highly intelligent, eloquent, a master of communication and learning, skilled in many sciences, beautiful in form, and resembles an elephant in gait (slow, deliberate, powerful). The native becomes a renowned scholar, astrologer, or advisor.

Hamsa Yoga: Jupiter in a kendra in Cancer (exaltation), Sagittarius, or Pisces (own signs). The native is righteous, wise, devoted to dharma, honored by kings and sages, handsome in appearance, and characterized by moral excellence. The native may become a religious leader, judge, or philosopher of great renown.

Malavya Yoga: Venus in a kendra in Taurus or Libra (own signs) or Pisces (exaltation). The native is beautiful, blessed with luxuries, artistic talent, and romantic success, fond of all pleasures, and may be wealthy and famous. The native has a youthful appearance and is gifted in the arts. Women with this yoga are particularly fortunate in marriage and social standing.

Shasha Yoga: Saturn in a kendra in Capricorn or Aquarius (own signs) or Libra (exaltation). The native rises through disciplined effort to positions of great authority, may be a manager of large enterprises or kingdoms, is devoted to service, and achieves fame through persistence and karmic right action. The native has a strong build and dark complexion, and may command armies or large organizations.

CHAPTER 22 — ADDITIONAL YOGAS FROM BRIHAT JATAKA TRADITION

Gajakesari Yoga: Jupiter in a kendra (1st, 4th, 7th, or 10th) from the Moon. This is one of the most widely known and important yogas in Jyotish. The native is brilliant, wise, prosperous, long-lived, and respected as an elephant (gaja) among persons — stately, powerful, and revered. The yoga is fully effective when Jupiter is free from malefic aspects and the Moon is also strong. Gajakesari natives are known for their eloquence, wisdom, social position, and the ability to inspire others.

Dhana Yogas (Wealth Combinations): The classical wealth yogas arise from connections between the lords of the wealth houses (2nd and 11th) and the trikona lords (1agna lord, 5th lord, 9th lord). When trikona lords exchange, conjoin, or mutually aspect the wealth lords, extraordinary wealth accumulation results. The most powerful Dhana Yoga is formed when the lagna lord and 2nd lord exchange signs (Parivartan Yoga) — the native earns exceptional wealth through personal initiative.

Viparita Raja Yoga: This unusual yoga is formed when the lords of the dusthana houses (6th, 8th, 12th) are in mutual exchange, conjunction, or aspect among themselves while these lords are placed in dusthana houses. The Viparita (reversed) effect means that what appears to be suffering (dusthana placement) actually produces remarkable results through unexpected reversals of fortune. The native rises through crises — others' downfall becoming the native's ascent. Classical examples: the 6th lord in the 8th, the 8th lord in the 12th, or the 12th lord in the 6th.

Neechabhanga Raja Yoga: (For further details, see Phaladeepika section.) The combination of debilitation with its cancellation creates one of the most powerful Raj Yogas. The Brihat Jataka tradition holds that a planet whose debility is cancelled produces results superior even to the same planet in its own sign, because the native has experienced and overcome the full weight of that planet's challenges.

Parivartana (Exchange) Yogas: When two planets occupy each other's signs (mutual exchange of signs), a powerful connection is created between the two planets and their respective house lordships. The exchange activates both houses simultaneously, interweaving their significations. An exchange between trikona lords (1st, 5th, 9th lords) creates a powerful dharma-trikona exchange that confers great fortune and spiritual merit. An exchange between a kendra lord and a trikona lord creates a Raja Yoga equivalent.

CHAPTER 23 — RASI DRISTI AND GRAHA DRISTI

Classical astrology distinguishes between two forms of planetary aspects:

Graha Dristi (Planetary Sight): Each planet has special aspects beyond the universal 7th house aspect:
- Saturn aspects the 3rd and 10th houses from itself (in addition to 7th)
- Jupiter aspects the 5th and 9th houses from itself (in addition to 7th)
- Mars aspects the 4th and 8th houses from itself (in addition to 7th)
- Sun, Moon, Mercury, and Venus have only the 7th house aspect

These special aspects are full-strength (100%) aspects. The 7th house aspect is also full strength. The special aspects of Saturn, Jupiter, and Mars are particularly important in chart analysis because they create strong connections between houses separated by these distances.

Rasi Dristi (Sign Sight): In Jaimini-influenced Brihat Jataka commentary, all moveable signs aspect all fixed signs (except the immediately adjacent one), and all fixed signs aspect all moveable signs. Dual (mutable) signs aspect each other mutually. This creates a web of sign-based aspects that operates somewhat differently from Graha Dristi and is particularly important in divisional chart analysis.

CHAPTER 24 — FINAL PRINCIPLES FROM BRIHAT JATAKA

The genius of Varahamihira's Brihat Jataka lies in its systematic, comprehensive approach to natal chart interpretation. The text lays the foundational architecture upon which all subsequent Jyotish texts build. Key final principles from the Brihat Jataka tradition:

The Varga (Divisional Chart) Principle: Beyond the rasi chart, Varahamihira establishes the importance of examining planetary positions in divisional charts (vargas). The Navamsha (D-9, ninth division) is the most important, showing the inner strength of planets and the deeper karmic dimensions of marriage, dharma, and destiny. A planet that appears weak in the rasi chart but is strong in the navamsha produces better results than expected. A planet strong in rasi but weakened in navamsha gives diminished results. The principle of Vargottama — a planet in the same sign in both the rasi and navamsha — gives that planet exceptional strength and stability.

Bhavat Bhavam Principle: The house that is the same number of houses away from any given house as that house is from the lagna shares similar significations with that original house. The 7th from the 7th is the 1st house — showing how the partner's perspective becomes the self. The 5th from the 5th is the 9th house — showing how children's children (grandchildren) connect to dharma and fortune. This principle allows the astrologer to extend the house analysis to deeper levels.

Synthesis in Judgment: The Brihat Jataka tradition emphasizes that no single factor can be judged in isolation. The lagna, lagna lord, the Moon sign, the Moon lord, the Sun sign, the relevant karaka, the bhava (house) concerned, and the Shad Bala (six-fold strength) of all involved planets must all be considered together before pronouncing any final result. An astrologer who judges from a single factor commits the greatest error. Only through synthesis — the weighing of all factors simultaneously — does the true picture of the native's destiny emerge.
